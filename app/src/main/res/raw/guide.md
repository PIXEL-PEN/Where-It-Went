
## Where It Went — Quick Guide

Where It Went helps you understand your spending without budgets, income tracking, or complexity.

You log what you spend. The app organizes it. That’s it.

---

### Daily Living

This is your everyday spending.

Entries are grouped by month and category. Each category has a tag:

* Fixed
* Necessity
* Discretionary
* Off-Budget

These tags power the Distribution graph, helping you see how your spending changes over time.

Off-Budget items are excluded from the graph — ideal for trips or special situations that shouldn’t distort normal life.

---

### Accounts

Accounts are for spending that doesn’t belong in normal monthly life.

You can create:

* Projects
* Travel
* Custom accounts

Each account stands on its own and does not affect Daily Living totals or the Distribution graph.

Accounts can be expanded or collapsed.
If a filter is active, a small ✕ indicator appears in the top bar — tap it to return to the full view.

Archived accounts remain visible in summaries but no longer accept new entries.

Deleting an account removes it together with all of its entries.

---

### Travel: spending in another currency

A Travel account can have its own currency. When you create it, turn on Foreign currency; for an existing trip use Manage Accounts → the trip → Currency….

Choose your home currency and the trip currency, then the rate. It is written the way banks show it, in whole numbers: 1 USD = 34 THB, 1 THB = 772 VND. A suggested rate is filled in (approximate, from the date shown); change it to your bank's or exchange booth's rate if you know it.

Enter amounts in the trip currency. Each entry shows the home value underneath, in whole numbers, and keeps the rate it was entered at, so changing the rate later only affects new entries. A trip whose home currency differs from Settings is shown in its own currency and kept out of mixed totals.

A trip can also simply be kept in another currency, with no conversion: choose the same currency for home and trip (for example US Dollar and US Dollar for a visit to the US while Settings stays on Dong). You enter dollars, the trip shows dollars, and it stays apart from your Dong totals. Daily Living and your other accounts carry on in the Settings currency.

### Travel mode

On a trip you can leave Daily Living aside. Menu → Travel mode → pick the trip.

The travel screen shows the trip day by day, with each day's total; tap a day to show or hide its entries. The card at the bottom takes new entries: item, amount, category, date and an optional note (the restaurant, tour or taxi). While you type, the card moves up out of the keyboard's way. Tap the rate line to change the rate.

While travel mode is on, the app opens on the travel screen with the same trip, category and date you used last. A date left on Today follows the calendar; a date you picked stays until you change it.

The home button takes you back to the main screen, where a ✈ button returns you to the trip. ✕ ends travel mode; the trip and its entries stay in Accounts.

---

### Journeys: trips across several countries

Each country is its own Travel account with its own currency. A journey groups them under one name, such as "SE Asia 2026".

On the travel screen, tap the trip name, then:

* Next country… starts the next country in the same journey, keeping its home currency. You only choose the new currency.
* Journey… puts the current trip in a journey, renames the journey, or (cleared) takes the trip out.

The top bar then shows the journey total in your home currency as well as the country's own total. A journey is only a grouping: entries always belong to a country's account, and removing the journey name deletes nothing.

### Yearly Summary

Open it from the menu, or from Summaries → Yearly summaries.

Each year has a banner with its total. Your year starts in the month of your first entry, so if you began in January you'll see January to December; if you began in May, May to April.

While a year is still in progress, the banner shows the months so far and when the year ends.

Tap a banner to open it. Daily Living and Accounts can each be opened to show their category totals, and each account can be opened to show its own categories.

Use Export › to save a year's totals as a CSV file or send them by email.

---

### Backup and Restore

Settings → Export → Full backup saves everything in one file: Daily Living, Accounts (with trip currencies, rates and journeys), your categories and their tags, and your currency. Keep a recent copy somewhere safe.

Settings → Import restores a full backup. It can also bring in an "All items" CSV export from Daily Living or Accounts.

Before importing, you'll see what the file contains. Entries already in the app are skipped and nothing is deleted, so importing the same file twice is harmless. A full backup also restores your category tags; CSV exports don't include tags, so categories they add start as Fixed.

Settings → Reset lets you delete Daily Living entries, Accounts, or both. Make a full backup first.

### Your Data

Everything stays on your device.

No ads.
No cloud.
No tracking.

Keep entries simple and consistent, and Where It Went will give you a clear picture of where your money went.

Feedback is always welcome: you'll find the email address in Settings → About.
